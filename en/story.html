Stories

Marechiaro,
a story of Ostia

On Lungomare Toscanelli, one of the historic beach clubs of Rome’s Lido. The story of a place that has seen generations of Roman summers, and of its new beginning.

1916 – the 1930s

When Rome discovered its sea

In antiquity Ostia was the port of Rome: goods from across the Mediterranean passed through here on their way to the capital of the empire, and the ruins of Ostia Antica, a few kilometres from the beach, still preserve its streets, baths and warehouses.

The modern Lido was born much later, in the early twentieth century, from an idea as simple as it was ambitious: to give Rome a sea of its own. In 1916 the first plan for the “seaside village” was drawn up. In the 1920s the Rome–Lido railway and the new road to the coast brought the city closer to the sea than ever before: the beach became a trip of a few hours, within everyone’s reach.

In 1924 the Roma beach establishment opened, among the largest and most luxurious in Europe; it was lost during the Second World War. In the 1930s the seafront filled with elegantly designed beach clubs, many of which still shape the profile of Ostia today.

After the war

The Romans’ sea

Rebuilt after the war, in the 1950s the coast lived its brightest season: holidays, society life, the cinema that captured it. Above all, though, Ostia became a habit, handed down from family to family.

The Sunday train, the beach hut, lunch facing the sea, the ride back to the city with the sun already low. For generations of Romans, “going to the sea” meant one thing only: Ostia.

Lungomare Toscanelli 27

Marechiaro

It is in this landscape that Marechiaro built its story. At number 27 on Lungomare Paolo Toscanelli, it is one of the historic beach clubs of Ostia: a name many Roman families link to the summers of their childhood, and one the press still counts among the most historic on the coast.

The photo archive Roma Sparita, devoted to the memory of the city, holds a vintage picture of the club. If you have a photograph taken here in your family album, we would love to receive it: Marechiaro’s story is also made of the memories of those who have passed through.

Since 2026

A new chapter

In 2026, with the renewal of the concessions on Rome’s coastline, Marechiaro passed to new management and reopened in July. Not to change what it is, but to bring it back to the standard its history deserves.

The work began with the essentials: a well-kept beach, well-spaced umbrellas, beach huts with shower and changing room. Then the kitchen, with a seafood restaurant open for lunch and dinner, and aperitivo at sunset. A historic place deserves to be lived from morning to night, and this is where Marechiaro starts again.
